A news article in _Nature_ (5 November 2026 issue) describes a simple screen for journals that may be engaging in questionable publishing. It is worth knowing before choosing where to submit a paper, or which papers to cite.

## The three signals

A preprint by Achal Agrawal and Moumita Koley proposes three warning signs, each checked for a journal in a single year:

1. its number of published papers doubles;
2. more than 70% of its papers come from one country;
3. at least 15% of its papers are flagged by the Problematic Paper Screener, a free online tool.

Applying these to journals listed in the open database OpenAlex, the authors found 3,753 journals that met at least one signal. Only 5% of those indexed in Scopus had been removed from it. Nearly 300 journals met two signals and 28 met all three, and the journals still listed published more than half a million papers in the years they were flagged.

## What the signals do not show

The signals point to journals that deserve a closer look; they do not prove misconduct. A new journal can grow quickly for ordinary reasons, and a small journal may naturally draw most of its authors from one country. The screen also misses many problems: only about one in five of the 1,075 journals that Scopus removed between 2015 and 2026 showed any of the three signs. Elsevier, which runs Scopus, says such signals are of limited use on their own and that each flagged journal is assessed individually.

## Citations after delisting

The same article reports a second preprint, by Álvaro Cabezas-Clavijo and Fernando Sánchez-Pita, which followed 509 journals removed from Scopus in 2018–2023. Removal reduced the citation rate for only about half of them, which suggests that researchers do not always check what they cite.

## For the lab

Before submitting to an unfamiliar journal, check whether it is indexed in Scopus or Web of Science, look at how its output has changed over the past few years, and talk it over with the group. The same care applies to the references in our own papers.
